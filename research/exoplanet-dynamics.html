01 / Overview

Overview

I study the dynamical evolution of low-mass planetary embryos undergoing disk-driven migration. In numerical experiments, convergent migration can lead to mean-motion resonance capture, resonant escape, orbital crossing, and collisions. These competing processes help determine whether compact systems remain in resonant chains or are reorganized by mergers.

02 / Background

Scientific context

Young planets exchange angular momentum with the surrounding gas disk. Differential migration changes neighboring period ratios and can bring planet pairs into mean-motion commensurabilities. Whether capture persists depends on migration and eccentricity-damping timescales, planetary mass ratios, and interactions with additional planets.

03 / Motivation

Research questions

  1. When is resonant capture stable, and when can librations become overstable?
  2. How do disk thickness, migration rate, and planetary mass ratio influence the pathway?
  3. What role do resonance crossing and embryo mergers play in final architectures?

04 / Approach

Methods & techniques

Numerical experiments

Integrate interacting embryos with REBOUND and REBOUNDx, including parameterized disk torques and eccentricity damping.

Resonance diagnostics

Track period ratios, eccentricities, resonant angles, and transitions between capture, libration, and escape.

Population comparisons

Compare runs across disk conditions and starting orbital spacings; examine final survivors and collision histories.
